About & how to use

A 2-minute primer on MURI and this tool

What is MURI?

MURI is a lean term meaning overburden — asking a person to do more than is reasonable. A MURI assessment measures how physically demanding each task is, so you can find and fix overburden before it wears people down or causes injury.

You rate every operation on nine posture and effort factors. Each is scored 3 worst, 2 middle, or 1 best. Added up they give a MURI score from 9 to 27 and a strain band: Low 9–10 Medium 11–12 High 13–27.

How to use it

  1. Fill in the position, assessor and date at the top.
  2. Add a task for each operation and give it a short description.
  3. Pick the nine ratings from the dropdowns — tap the ? on any factor to see a diagram.
  4. Enter the cycle time and how many times it repeats per shift (frequency), and add a photo if you have one.

The dashboard updates as you go. Use Save project to keep your work, Report / PDF to share it.

Why frequency matters

Strain alone doesn't tell the whole story: a mild task repeated hundreds of times a shift can harm a worker more than a hard task done twice. So the tool combines how hard (strain band) with how often (frequency) into a priority — the classic risk = severity × exposure idea. Frequency bands are rules of thumb you can read against your own process:

Low < 100 reps/shift  •  Medium 100–400  •  High > 400.

Critical and High priority tasks are where to act first. The exposure-weighted score on the dashboard also leans on frequency — long, frequent tasks pull the number up more than quick, rare ones.

This tool is a screening aid to help prioritise, not a medical or legal determination. For confirmed problems, follow up with a qualified ergonomist.
